To me, systems are not software architectures—they are the cognitive and structural frameworks I design to optimize human execution.

As an avid learner, I try to view productivity and academic mastery as engineering problems that require deliberate design. This repository documents the exact behavioral protocols, memory synthesis systems, and minimalist environments I have engineered to eliminate cognitive friction, maintain absolute academic focus, and systematically achieve top-tier metrics throughout high school.

What do I use for academics?

It is called blurting, and the idea is simple. Read your notes for 30 minutes, then write down everything you remember on paper. Reread to fill any memory gaps until you know it perfectly. This method transformed my academic performance starting freshman year. If you are in high school, or even in uni, I highly encourage you to try it. Also, I've started to use Field Notes (Heavy Duty Edition) as one of my main workbooks, will see how it works in the long run.

What do I use for productivity?

To be more productive with my time, I switched my phone from a colorful, dopamine-filled homescreen to a minimalist, dumb-phone setup. It has worked wonders for me ever since I've started using it in my everyday life. I also use apps like ScreenZen to help me restrict social media during certain hours of the day. Before this, I was plagued by the need to doom-scroll every waking minute of the day. After the change, however, I've slowly but surely started to do away with that habit. And it only takes around 20 mins to set up.

I also highly encourage you to use this setup as your daily-driver. Obviously, this isn't the only productive tool I use, but it is by far the most important.
