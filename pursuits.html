I think of my favorite pursuits not as separate hobbies, but as different ways to challenge my mind and stay grounded. My main focus is software engineering and data science—building tools that make sense of messy, real-world data. But I balance that technical world by diving into literature, writing, and watching competitive football.

To me, these worlds aren't isolated. The pattern recognition and tactical strategy I see when analyzing a football play, the deep focus I get from a tough book, and the clarity I find in daily journaling all feed right back into how I write code. They help me look at data landscapes not just as numbers, but as puzzles waiting for creative, structured solutions.

▶Football

I'm a football fan, or as some would say "soccer". I support Liverpool F.C. and watch their games in the Premier League, Champions League and other competitions whenever I have the time. I got into football around the 6th grade and have been following it closely ever since.

Under Fenway Sports Group, Liverpool famously became the pioneer of heavy data analytics in global sports. They treat football like a computer science problem—optimizing spatial geometry, tracking passing matrices, and using predictive models to scout undervalued talent like Mo Salah. As someone interested in CS, supporting LFC means appreciating efficiency algorithms and system optimization playing out in real-time on grass, as well as the tight-knit community in Merseyside and in Gondar.

▶Programming

It’s probably obvious by now, but I love tinkering with technology and building new things from scratch. Honestly, the main reason I built this entire website was just to put my coding skills to the test and see what I could create on my own. While this site gave me a solid foundation, I see it as just the starting point.

I’m planning to dive deeper into lower-level mechanics and data scripting next, expanding my toolkit into languages like C++, Python, and more advanced JavaScript to build faster, more complex systems.

▶Journaling

I started journaling a few months ago, and honestly, it’s been incredibly refreshing to get my thoughts out of my head and onto a piece of paper. Whenever I have a lot on my mind, I just jot down what's important. Taking that breather helps me look back and reflect on what I was thinking with a lot more clarity. It’s become a simple but essential way for me to slow down, organize my goals, and stay grounded.

The habits of journaling and tracking my days became so engrained in my routine that I decided to take it to the next level: I created an entire programming language for it. Built purely for the fun of exploration, Inkwell is an esoteric scripting language that lets you log your thoughts and map your lifestyle metrics directly inside the terminal. You can download it for macOS or Windows, give it a try!

▶Reading

I wouldn’t call this my biggest interest by any means, but I do like reading from time to time—whether it’s a regular book or the Bible. Honestly, it's actually a good way to step away from screens, get my mind off of things, and find some peace and quiet. But beyond that, reading gives me a chance to slow down and appreciate how stories are put together.

Whether I'm looking at a modern novel or diving into the historical texts and moral lessons of scripture, I like seeing how centuries-old writings can still teach us about human behavior and connect people across generations today.
